Short notes should stay operational. A title, a date, and a few lines are enough when the goal is to preserve context instead of produce an essay.

The rail is only helpful if it gets out of the way. That is why I prefer the note format to stay small and the navigation to stay obvious. The thing should help me recover a thought, not become the thought.

This fits with [Keeping notes small](/writing/notes/keeping-notes-small) and [Disposable observations](/writing/notes/disposable-observations), which together describe a tiny ecosystem: short capture, quick retrieval, and no expectation that every note will become a chapter.

I also think this is part of what makes [Pebbles](/projects/pebbles) interesting. If a system is going to collect a lot of small fragments, the rails and indexes around those fragments need to be almost invisible.

Outside the site, [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) and [Logseq](https://logseq.com/) are useful references for the range of possible note-taking ergonomics, even if I do not want to copy their complexity wholesale.
