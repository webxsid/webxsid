title: Keeping notes small
summary: Notes should be fast to write and easy to forget.

Small notes work best when they capture one idea and stop there. That keeps them light enough to write in the moment and light enough to revisit later without feeling like a project in their own right.

The more a note tries to do, the less likely it is to survive the moment it was created for. I prefer them to stay close to the shape of a reminder, a quote, a decision, or a next step.

That is why I keep [Rails for quick thoughts](/writing/notes/rails-for-quick-thoughts) nearby as a companion piece. Together, the two notes create a small system: one for short-lived fragments, one for the structure that keeps those fragments findable.

[Pebbles](/projects/pebbles) pushes on the same idea from another angle, because a captured fragment only matters if the system around it stays quick enough to accept the next one.

External reading: [The Verge on note-taking overload](https://www.theverge.com/2021/3/3/22311806/note-taking-productivity-apps-notion-obsidian-roam) and [Andy Matuschak on note making](https://andymatuschak.org/), mostly because they both reinforce that a note is a working object, not a polished artifact.
